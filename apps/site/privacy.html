Privacy

Spelling and grammar checks run on your device. Optional cloud AI sends text only when you enable it and request a check. GrammarMe has no accounts, analytics or ads, and the project never sees what you write.

Grammar and spelling checks

The checker runs on your computer. Your text is not uploaded, logged or stored anywhere except your own browser.

What is kept in your browser

  • Your settings, personal dictionary and turned-off rules.
  • Your current draft, if “Keep my draft in this browser” is on. You can delete it in Settings at any time.
  • In the extension, where you moved GrammarMe's button on each site. This stays on your device.
  • The extension's on/off switches, turned-off sites and dialect use Chrome's sync storage, so they follow your Chrome profile if Chrome sync is on.

The optional AI check

It is off until you turn it on and add your own server in Settings. Text is sent only when you press AI check, up to 4,000 characters at a time, to that server and then to the AI provider it is set up with. Each provider has its own terms; the project notes compare them.

The Chrome extension

The extension checks text boxes on the pages you visit, on your computer. It skips password, one-time-code and payment fields. Its spelling and grammar checks send nothing anywhere.

The optional ChatGPT check (extension only)

It is off until you install the GrammarMe companion on your computer and choose Continue with ChatGPT. You sign in on OpenAI's own page. Your ChatGPT credentials are stored only by the companion, in a file on your computer that only your user account can read. They are never stored in the browser or sent to the GrammarMe project.

Text is sent only when you press Send to ChatGPT after previewing it: up to 4,000 characters, from the extension to the companion and then to OpenAI, using your ChatGPT plan. OpenAI's terms apply to that text. Disconnect in the extension at any time to revoke the session and delete the credentials. How it works

Hosting and fonts

This website and the editor are served by GitHub Pages, which receives your IP address when you load them, like any web host. What you type in the editor is never sent to it.

This website loads its fonts from Google Fonts, which receives your IP address when the page loads. The editor itself loads no third-party fonts.
